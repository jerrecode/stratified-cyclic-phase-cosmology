\section{Layered topology and higher-dimensional demonstrator}
\subsection{$S^4$ foliation versus cyclic FLRW}
The round four-sphere has the Riemannian metric
\begin{equation}
d\ell^2=R^2\left(d\chi^2+\sin^2\chi\,d\Omega_3^2\right),\qquad 0\le\chi\le\pi.
\end{equation}
Each fixed-$\chi$ leaf is an $S^3$ with areal radius
\begin{equation}
A(\chi)=R\sin\chi.
\end{equation}
Its increase from zero to $R$ and decrease back to zero is a statement about a spatial foliation. It is not a cosmological expansion followed by contraction because $\chi$ is spatial in this geometry and no Lorentzian evolution equation has been supplied.

The global topology also differs. If a regular Lorentzian closed-FLRW solution has $a(t)>0$ throughout and time is periodically identified, its topology is $S^3\times S^1$, not $S^4$. Without periodic time identification it is $S^3\times I$. Collapsing $S^3$ leaves at endpoints can produce a smooth $S^4$ only in a properly regular Euclidean construction; a Lorentzian endpoint with $a=0$ is ordinarily a big-bang or big-crunch singularity rather than a smooth pole by analogy.

\subsection{Causal scalar propagation on simultaneous layers}
To make the statement that all spatial leaves exist simultaneously precise without inventing a gravity theory, we use the fixed $4+1$ dimensional spacetime
\begin{equation}
ds^2=-dt^2+R^2\left(d\chi^2+\sin^2\chi\,d\Omega_3^2\right)
\end{equation}
and an $S^3$-invariant test scalar $\varphi(t,\chi)$. With
$U(\varphi)=m^2\varphi^2/2+\lambda\varphi^4/4$, its equation is
\begin{equation}
\partial_t^2\varphi+\gamma\partial_t\varphi-
\frac{1}{R^2\sin^3\chi}\partial_\chi\!\left(\sin^3\chi\,\partial_\chi\varphi\right)
+m^2\varphi+\lambda\varphi^3=0.
\end{equation}
Regularity gives zero radial flux at both poles. The finite-volume cell measure is evaluated exactly,
\begin{equation}
w_i=\int_{\chi_{i-1/2}}^{\chi_{i+1/2}}\sin^3\chi\,d\chi
=\left[-\cos\chi+\frac13\cos^3\chi\right]_{\chi_{i-1/2}}^{\chi_{i+1/2}},
\end{equation}
and the face flux is proportional to
$\sin^3\chi_{i+1/2}(\varphi_{i+1}-\varphi_i)/\Delta\chi$. For $\gamma=0$ the energy diagnostic uses the same face-gradient quadratic form as the discrete Laplacian. The continuum expression is
\begin{equation}
E=2\pi^2R^4\int_0^\pi d\chi\,\sin^3\chi\left[
\frac12\dot\varphi^2+\frac{(\partial_\chi\varphi)^2}{2R^2}+U(\varphi)\right].
\end{equation}
This matching between operator and diagnostic is essential; an unrelated pointwise gradient would not test conservation of the semi-discrete system.

\subsection{Why a fully interacting $4+1$ model is not yet computed}
Mass-energy cannot consistently ``gravitate on every layer'' until the model specifies at least a five-dimensional action, the five-dimensional Planck scale, field content and couplings, topology and boundary conditions, and a gauge/constraint formulation. A legitimate numerical-relativity implementation would evolve the spatial metric and extrinsic curvature while enforcing Hamiltonian and momentum constraints. The present four-dimensional potential cannot uniquely determine those ingredients, and fitting unspecified forces to manufacture a stable configuration would be circular. The fixed-background equation above is therefore the sharp boundary of what follows from the currently declared higher-dimensional definitions.
